# Vision

**MAGNET SWEEP** — *Find it. Pull it. Bring it home. Bolt it on.*

## The pitch

You start with a magnet that can barely lift a screw. You end up dragging an excavator bucket out of a
factory while a security bulldozer chases you, so you can bolt it onto a robot made of a fridge door and
a STOP sign, and let that robot fight for control of an arena while you go back for something even
stupider.

## The player fantasy

Early:

> "I started by collecting screws. Now I'm running away from a security robot while dragging an engine
> behind me so I can put it inside my homemade fighting robot."

Later:

> "My robot has an excavator arm, a satellite head, car-door armour and a nuclear-looking power core,
> and it has controlled the arena for eight minutes."

## The five layers

Each one exists to make the next one matter.

1. **Sweep** — the ASMR layer. Objects shake, tilt, spark, lift, spiral in and land with a *tik*. This
   has to be satisfying on its own, before any progression is attached to it. If sweeping is not
   pleasant, nothing downstream saves the game.
2. **Go deeper** — twelve tiers, gated on Magnet Power. Forward means bigger, heavier, rarer, more
   dangerous. Depth is legible by walking.
3. **Extract** — some objects are **Robot Parts**. Ripping one free trips a Salvage Breach and turns the
   walk home into a 20-45 second chase while the thing you are carrying slows you down.
4. **Build** — a secured part is physically bolted onto your robot. Not "Robot Level 25". A traffic
   light, a fridge door, a spoon.
5. **Arena** — the robot fights automatically for the Arena Core, takes damage, and needs scrap to
   repair.

## The pinch

Everything converges on one decision:

> **Do I spend this scrap on myself, or on keeping my robot alive?**

150 scrap is either 3,600 Coins toward a better magnet, or +900 robot HP. The game never resolves this
for the player, and that tension is the reason the loop repeats.

## What this is not

- **Not a pet simulator.** There is no multiplier treadmill dressed as content.
- **Not a magnet simulator.** Collection is the *input* to a build game, not the whole game.
- **Not a robot fighting game.** You never control the robot in combat. You control what it is made of.
- **Not a loot-box game.** Parts exist physically in the world. You go and get them.

## The one question that decides whether it works

Section 84 of the spec, and the gate for the whole project:

> **When the player sees a strange object in the distance, do they think "I want that on my robot"?**

If yes, the core works and everything else is content. If no, more zones will not fix it.
